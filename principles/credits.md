# Credits: what this borrows, and what changed

This OS stands on work that was shared generously. Credit where it's due, specifically.

## Nate B. Jones: the Open family

- **Open Brain** (the memory argument: your AI memory should be yours, portable and inspectable) shapes the brain room, and the wider habit of keeping memory outside any one tool. https://natesnewsletter.substack.com
- **Open Skills** (procedures as portable, owned work packages: trigger, boundary, proof; not prompts trapped in one app) shapes the skills room and the whole skills-in-git approach.
- **Open Engine** (the task record that lets one AI's work become the next AI's job, with receipts: AGENT CLAIMED, BLOCKED, DONE) is the engine room, close to verbatim in spirit. The seven-part task record and the receipt vocabulary are his.

**What changed:** the rooms wrap these in a single visual metaphor (the campus), bind them to the honesty rules, and add the pointing rule: the OS shows what needs the human and links out; the systems of record keep the records.

## Ankit Patel

- His **AI Architecture deep dive** (shared in Nate's Exec Circle, June 2026); tier every surface, bound every brain, prove every output, and draw the whole stack in layers: is the direct ancestor of the flow room. The campus map is that architecture redrawn as a place.
- His **Make It Make Sense protocol** (visible routing, trust markers on claims, insight filters) influenced how honestly this OS talks about what it knows and doesn't.

## Brian Proffitt and the Exec Circle

- Brian's pricing-psychology playbook is the template for how a specialist's skill becomes a reusable card in someone else's engine.
- The community habit of sharing working systems: not advice, actual working systems: is why this repo exists at all.

## Mark Bunce and ashton-papi: the contract layer

Both arrived within a day of this repo going public, from different directions, at the same conclusion: the prose serves humans well, and agents need a thin machine-readable contract underneath it.

- **Mark Bunce** (building his own Whole Life OS) sent the sharpest single line of feedback this repo has received: *"The strongest advice is not 'make it bigger.' It is: preserve the clarity, then add just enough contract underneath it so agents can operate safely."* The campus manifest ([campus.yaml](../campus.yaml)), the six-word confidence vocabulary in the honesty rules, and the proof gates in the first week are his suggestions, adopted nearly as given.
- **ashton-papi** pressure-tested the permissions story: what can each agent read, what can it write, which doors need a human signature, and how does the system show stale or missing data without pretending it knows? The keycards and confidence words in the manifest are the answer his questions demanded.

**What changed:** their advice arrived as six suggestions; three went in. Receipts already had a template, the source-of-truth register folded into the manifest rather than becoming its own file, and dashboard-sprawl protection was already rule-shaped. The first version of this OS died of complication; saying no to good ideas is part of the pattern.

## Limited Edition Jonathan: the sessions room

Jonathan, in Nate's WhatsApp group, was building a session board plugin: one place to see every AI session you have open and which ones are stuck. Seeing it was the moment the sessions room became obvious. I didn't install his plugin; I borrowed the question it answers and built the plainest version I could from the files the Claude desktop app already writes, with no model calls and a privacy list it won't run without.

**What changed:** the board became a list with four bands, the raised hand is the app's own signal, and the whole thing is a Python script on a five-minute schedule. The idea is his; the restraint is the house style.

## What's original here

The campus metaphor as a whole (buildings, colleagues, keycards, the gatehouse, the pillars), the honesty rules as written, the green (a room that protects the human, with a charter the system obeys), agents named as people on a floor, and the insistence that every page can say "I don't know".
